## M. Constraint as a method (16)

### 1. Paired constraints, Stokes · 2006 · ✓ confirmed

Stokes models creativity as paired constraints: one precludes the reliable, successful solution, the other promotes a novel substitute, and each must be specified. Her Monet example bars dark-light contrasts and promotes contrasting closely valued colours.

- **For the loop:** Each performer's constraint should come as a pair, ban the habitual move and name its replacement, and the pair should follow from the score's goal, not be picked arbitrarily.
- **Links:** knobs, signature, libex
- **Opened:** abstract + preface excerpt — https://doi.org/10.1037//0003-066x.56.4.355 ; https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780826197856_A26559201/preview-9780826197856_A26559201.pdf
- **Fact-check note:** Opened preface excerpt (pageplace PDF), Stokes 2008 abstract. Preface text matches the detail. Year: Stokes cites the book as 2006 herself; book is copyright 2005/2006.

### 2. Rats and the clinamen, Oulipo · 1960 · ◐ narrower

Oulipo (founded 1960) used formal constraints; the group's early definition of its members was 'rats who have to build the labyrinth from which they plan to escape'. One commentator calls the clinamen, a deliberate swerve from a restriction, their only concession; Perec's missing 100th chapter is the example.

- **For the loop:** A performer's break from a banned move should be logged as a named deviation with the compliant option it rejected, so a clinamen is a recorded choice rather than drift.
- **Links:** knobs, plainuni, diff
- **Opened:** secondary — https://www.rochester.edu/College/translation/threepercent/2012/09/10/reading-in-reverse-part-i-of-iii/ ; https://doi.org/10.1080/13528165.2016.1192866 ; https://www.ub.edu/llulldb/docs/Calvino.pdf
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Bray 2016 article and the 1961 Oulipo meeting minutes (French original of the rat definition). Changed: quote is a translation of the 1961 minutes; 'only concession' is Bray's gloss, not the group's; Perec example clarified (book has 99 chapters where the structure implies 100). 'Often mathematical' not checked.

### 3. Less is more in art, Elster · 2000 · ✗ corrected

Elster's chapter 'Less Is More' treats artists imposing constraints on themselves: Perec's e-less novel is entirely idiosyncratic, while genre conventions are freely chosen but not invented by the artist. Elster also notes an imposed constraint can become chosen, as black-and-white film did.

- **For the loop:** Mark each constraint on a score line by origin (imposed by the brief, chosen from a genre, or invented for this round), since they ask different things of a performer.
- **Links:** knobs, plainuni
- **Opened:** abstract/summary — https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/ulysses-unbound/less-is-more-creativity-and-constraints-in-the-arts/4BC88D7CD2A13CF8B1D7305030F16CA2 ; secondary quote of Elster in https://www.hasegawa.research.mcgill.ca/pdf/Hasegawa-Creating_with_Constraints_2022.pdf
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Cambridge chapter summary and Hasegawa 2022, which cites Elster 2000 p.4 for the black-and-white point. Corrected: the original attributed that point to 'a commentator'; it is Elster's own. Chapter text itself not read beyond the summary.

### 4. Constraints as freedom, Stravinsky · 1947 · ✓ confirmed

In Poetics of Music Stravinsky says his freedom grows the more narrowly he limits his field and surrounds himself with obstacles: 'The more constraints one imposes, the more one frees one's self of the chains that shackle the spirit.' Cited to p. 65 (1947 edition).

- **For the loop:** This is a statement from a composer, not evidence; use it as motivation for tighter score lines, and test the effect with the swatch run.
- **Links:** knobs, plainuni
- **Opened:** secondary quoting the book — https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674302433 ; https://www.hasegawa.research.mcgill.ca/pdf/Hasegawa-Creating_with_Constraints_2022.pdf ; reading notes at jazzvideolessons.net
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Hasegawa 2022 (cites Stravinsky 1947, 65), a notes PDF marking the passage at p. 65, and the HUP page quoting it. Quote and page match; page depends on edition (a quote site cites a 1970 edition, also p. 65).

### 5. Obstructions as a game, von Trier & Leth · 2003 · ✓ confirmed

Von Trier makes Leth remake his 1967 short The Perfect Human five times under 'obstructions' (twelve-frame shots in Cuba; a miserable place not shown; a cartoon). Because Leth fails the second, the third becomes 'complete freedom or back to Bombay'.

- **For the loop:** Each next constraint can be chosen after reading the diff on the previous round, as a sequence of obstructions, with a 'complete freedom' round as a deliberate control.
- **Links:** divdec, diff, plan
- **Opened:** secondary — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Five_Obstructions ; https://www.dfi.dk/en/viden-om-film/filmdatabasen/film/de-fem-benspaend
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Wikipedia and FIPRESCI review. Obstruction sequence matches. The fifth remake is written by von Trier. Secondary sources only.

### 6. Ten rules of chastity, Dogme 95 · 1995 · ✓ confirmed

The Vow of Chastity lists ten rules: location shooting, sound with image, hand-held camera, colour, no optical work or filters, no superficial action, here and now, no genre films, Academy 35 mm, no director credit. It also vows to refrain from personal taste.

- **For the loop:** A set of refusals gives a recognisable look (a new uniform) and was bent in practice, so list its breaks next to it, as the plain-arm result with 17 of 17 white pages suggests.
- **Links:** plainuni, subtract
- **Opened:** primary text — https://dogme95.com/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogme_95
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the manifesto text at dogme95.com. All ten rules and the personal-taste vow are there; signed 13 March 1995.

### 7. Cards for being stuck, Eno & Schmidt · 1975 · ✓ confirmed

Oblique Strategies is a deck of cards, each a short lateral prompt such as 'Honour thy error as a hidden intention' or 'Use an old idea', drawn at an impasse. Eno and Schmidt first published it in 1975, 500 copies, 113 cards.

- **For the loop:** A drawn random prompt is a cheap knob for a stuck performer, but it is a random constraint, so keep it separate from the paired constraints that come from the score.
- **Links:** knobs, signature
- **Opened:** secondary — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies ; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/sep/07/oblique-strategies
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Wikipedia (edition table: 1975, 113 cards, 500 copies) and the Oblique Strategies FAQ (card one: printed January 1975, edition of 500; Eno quote on the first strategy).

### 8. Dropping a constraint, Boden · 1990 · ✓ confirmed

Boden separates combinational, exploratory and transformational creativity. In the transformational kind the conceptual space itself is changed, for example by dropping a defining constraint; her example is Schoenberg dropping the home-key constraint of tonal music.

- **For the loop:** Separate rounds that explore within the current score from rounds that deliberately drop one defining line, and say which one a round was.
- **Links:** knobs, libex
- **Opened:** abstract + secondary — https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x0003569x ; https://www.cse.msu.edu/~cse841/papers/Boden.html ; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1609/aimag.v30i3.2254
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Boden's online text (dropping home key; dropping a constraint as heuristic) and a Sussex paper noting the three forms appear in later editions. The 1990 book is the origin; the three-way split is stated in later editions and her 1994/1998 writings.

### 9. Restricted choice, Sellier & Dahl · 2011 · ✓ confirmed

In two studies (knitting and crafting), restricting the choice of creative inputs raised the creativity experts judged for experienced consumers, who enjoyed the process more. Everyone, regardless of experience, felt more creative with more choice.

- **For the loop:** How creative a performer feels with a wide score says nothing about its output, so judge the output blind and don't ask the performer.
- **Links:** knobs, plainuni
- **Opened:** abstract — https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0407 ; working paper https://web-docs.stern.nyu.edu/pa/sellier_dahl_creative_success.pdf
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the JMR abstract (doi) and the NYU working paper abstract; matches. Abstract only.

### 10. Examples early and repeatedly, Kulkarni, Dow & Klemmer · 2012 · ✓ confirmed

In an online experiment (N=81), people drew animals for an alien Earth-like planet. Exposure to examples raised conformity; early exposure improved creativity, repeated exposure interspersed with prototyping did even better, and late exposure raised conformity without improving creativity.

- **For the loop:** A Library example should be shown early in the round and again after the first sketch, and the diff should count features copied from it as conformity.
- **Links:** libex, diagram
- **Opened:** full text excerpt — https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2012/RepeatedExamplesCogSci2012/RepeatedExamplesCogSci2012.pdf
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the Stanford PDF (abstract and results). Matches.

### 11. Structured imagination, Ward · 1994 · ◐ narrower

Ward's five experiments with 385 undergraduates imagining animals from another galaxy support the idea that similar structures and processes underlie creative and noncreative cognition. Imagined animals tended to keep typical Earth-animal properties (the paper's 'characteristic properties').

- **For the loop:** A model asked for something new will likely carry over the defaults of its category, which fits the shared look; the ask must name which structure to break.
- **Links:** libex, unsaid, avgtaste
- **Opened:** abstract (ERIC) + secondary — https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ494059 ; https://escholarship.org/content/qt64n3300n/qt64n3300n_noSplash_94c4dbd3f431be08121186012c4f68c8.pdf
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the ERIC record (5 experiments, 385 undergraduates, quoted conclusion). The bilateral-symmetry example from a later paper was not checked and is dropped; 'keep typical properties' is the standard reading of the paper's title and abstract wording but only the ERIC abstract was read.

### 12. Generate, then explore, Finke, Ward & Smith · 1992 · ◐ narrower

Geneplore (Finke, Ward & Smith 1992) has a generative phase producing preinventive structures and an exploratory phase interpreting them. In Finke's studies, forming the shape before learning its category gave the most creative but fewer practical inventions, by cross-experiment comparison.

- **For the loop:** Ask performers for rough preinventive sketches before naming the target, then interpret them against the score; the plan-first step can run in that order.
- **Links:** plan, sampled, divdec
- **Opened:** secondary — https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/psychology/geneplore-model ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8906409/ ; MIT Press page https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262061506/creative-cognition/
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Dix et al. 2006 summary of Geneplore and Finke's preinventive-form studies, and Smith, Ward & Schumacher 1993 intro. Changed: 'more stereotyped' reworded to what the secondary source says (more creative, fewer practical), flagged as cross-experiment comparison. Book not opened.

### 13. Keep, break, leave, Shu-ha-ri · 18th century · ◐ narrower

Shu-ha-ri names three stages of learning in Japanese arts: keep the teacher's form, break it, leave it. The three characters appear in the writings of Kawakami Fuhaku (1719-1807); a verse handed down as Sen no Rikyu's ends 'do not forget the root'.

- **For the loop:** Treat a junior performer's first rounds as keep-the-form work and mark which score lines may be broken only after that; do not cite Rikyu as the origin.
- **Links:** rung, prov
- **Opened:** secondary — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu-ha-ri ; https://sumimasenguide.com/articles/shuhari-and-zen ; https://www.jka.or.jp/en/monthly-column/2-from-art-to-pathkusahara-katsuhide-chairman-of-jka-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2/
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Sumimasen Guide and a note.com explainer. Fuhaku dates and the Rikyu verse match; the guide says Rikyu's authorship of the verse is unconfirmed, and the exact date of Fuhaku's text is not given, so year is approximate. Secondary sources.

### 14. Transmission by copying, Xie He · 550 · ✓ confirmed

Xie He's six principles of painting, from the preface to his Record of the Classification of Old Painters (c. 550), include as the sixth 'Transmission by Copying': copying of models, not only from life but also from the works of antiquity.

- **For the loop:** Copying a Library part is an accepted first rung, and the provenance mark should say copied; do not equate copying with the model reusing its own defaults.
- **Links:** rung, libex
- **Opened:** secondary — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_principles_of_Chinese_painting ; https://ulearninglabs.umag.hku.hk/en/dictionaries/huihua-liufa/ ; Wang Keping abstract on cnki
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Wikipedia, ctext and HKU ULearning Labs; wording matches. Secondary sources; the principle is terse in the original and translations vary.

### 15. Rasa as flavour, Natyashastra · c. 200 BCE-200 CE · ✓ confirmed

In the Natyashastra rasa is produced from a combination of determinants, consequents and transitory states, and is relished like a dish made from combined ingredients. It lists eight rasas tied to eight durable states such as love, mirth and fear.

- **For the loop:** A persona or score line can state the state to be evoked in the viewer as well as the form, which fits the Expects and Just before lines.
- **Links:** pscore, plainlines
- **Opened:** primary translation + secondary — https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-natyashastra/d/doc209700.html ; https://riviste.unimi.it/index.php/itinera/en/article/download/8721/8294/25937

### 16. A list of refusals, Rainer · 1965 · ✓ confirmed

Rainer closed a 1965 essay on her dance Parts of Some Sextets with the No Manifesto: no to spectacle, virtuosity, transformations and magic, the star image, the heroic, style, camp, eccentricity, and moving or being moved, among others.

## N. Tolerances, defaults and specs (17)

### 17. Bands and stack-ups, GD&T · 2007 · ◐ narrower

A dimension is allowed a tolerance zone, and the unspecified distance across an assembly accumulates the contributors' variations. Worst-case analysis guarantees assembly but is costly; relaxing to, say, 99.97% acceptance with statistical analysis allows looser tolerances. (Shah et al., CAD&A 2007.)

- **For the loop:** A 'band' line should carry its width, and the spread of several lines read together has to be checked as a stack, not line by line.
- **Links:** statesgap, swatch, signature
- **Opened:** secondary - Piegl, 'Navigating the Tolerance Analysis Maze', CAD&A 4(5) 2007 (cad-journal.net/files/vol_4/CAD_4(5)_2007_705-718.pdf); abstract of Fischer, Worst-Case Tolerance Analysis. ASME Y14.5 text itself not read
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the paper PDF (cad-journal.net). Wording 'say 99.97% acceptance' matches the paper. Citation fix: authors are Shah, Ameta, Shen, Davidson, not Piegl (Piegl is the journal editor). A separate source gives 99.73% for the usual 3-sigma case, so 99.97% is the paper's illustrative figure.

### 18. Sets narrowed gradually, Sobek, Ward & Liker · 1999 · ✓ confirmed

Toyota's set-based approach starts by broadly considering sets of possible solutions in parallel, then gradually narrows them to converge; the authors give three principles, the last being establishing feasibility before commitment.

- **For the loop:** Letting performers return sets of variants and narrowing lines stepwise (open, then band, then fixed) matches this; the default is to commit to one point early.
- **Links:** plan, divdec, statesgap
- **Opened:** abstract and opening of the article - Sloan Management Review 40(2), Winter 1999 (sloanreview.mit.edu; abstract via dialnet.unirioja.es)
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the Dialnet abstract and the opening of the MIT Sloan Management Review page. Matches.

### 19. Last responsible moment, Poppendieck · 2003 · ◐ narrower

The Poppendiecks define the last responsible moment as the moment at which failing to make a decision eliminates an important alternative; past it, decisions get made by default. They stress that delaying decisions is hard work, not procrastination.

- **For the loop:** An 'open' line needs a stated trigger, the point at which leaving it open removes an option; without one it silently becomes a 'default'.
- **Links:** statesgap, divdec, oneq
- **Opened:** full text of Lean Software Development ch. 3 sample pages (ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780321150783/samplepages/0321150783.pdf); quote repeated at codinghorror.com
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Mary Poppendieck's 2003 essay (exact definition) and the book sample pages. Dropped the claim that they credit the Lean Construction Institute: the sample pages only thank LCI staff; the LCI glossary has its own, different wording of LRM.

### 20. Control vs noise factors, Taguchi · 1980s · ◐ narrower

Robust parameter design treats design parameters as control factors and assesses robustness under noise factors. ISO 16336:2014, based on Taguchi Methods, defines robust as insensitivity of a product's function to changes in noise levels.

- **For the loop:** Treat the brief and score as control factors and the model and run-to-run variation as noise; judge a score by whether its output holds when the performer changes.
- **Links:** swatch, signature, m56
- **Opened:** abstracts - Wu & Hamada, Introduction to Robust Parameter Design (Wiley 2021); Robinson, Borror & Myers, Quality & Reliability Eng. Intl 2003; ISO 16336:2014 page
- **Fact-check note:** Opened ISO 16336:2014 abstract and sample text. Dropped the 'popularised by Taguchi in mid-1980s' claim and the Wu & Hamada reference: the date is unverified (year field is approximate).

### 21. Visible rules, hidden parameters, Baldwin & Clark · 2000 · ◐ narrower

In a modular design, 'visible' modules carry design rules that hidden modules must obey; design decisions inside a hidden module affect no other module. Designers can replace early solutions there with better ones later, which Baldwin and Clark model as real options.

- **For the loop:** Fixed lines act as visible rules for all performers; open lines are the hidden part where performers may differ without breaking the whole.
- **Links:** statesgap, diagram, specgap
- **Opened:** secondary - Baldwin & Clark, 'Modularity after the Crash' (hbs.edu) and their chapter 'Modularity in the Design of Complex Engineering Systems'; book itself not opened
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Baldwin & Clark, 'Modularity after the Crash' (HBS) and the MIT Press blurb. Dropped 'main source of option value': that essay says options exist in modules and gives technical potential, experiment cost and visibility as drivers. Book itself not opened.

### 22. Hide what will change, Parnas · 1972 · ✓ confirmed

Parnas argues that decomposing a system into modules from a flowchart is almost always incorrect; instead begin with a list of difficult design decisions or ones likely to change, and design each module to hide one such decision from the others.

- **For the loop:** Mark on the score which decisions are likely to change and keep them out of what other parts depend on.
- **Links:** specgap, statesgap, divdec
- **Opened:** full text - Communications of the ACM 15(12), Dec 1972 (wstomv.win.tue.nl copy; abstract at dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/361598.361623)
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the Parnas 1972 paper text (conclusion and criteria sections) via ACM and Colorado State copies. Matches.

### 23. Ambiguity poll, Gause & Weinberg · 1989 · ◐ narrower

Exploring Requirements (1989) covers an ambiguity poll (chapters 4, 19) and a 'Mary had a little lamb' heuristic: say a requirement aloud, stressing each word in turn, and ask what it could mean.

- **For the loop:** Count how many distinct readings independent performers give, and use the stress-each-word trick on a score line to write the questions it implies.
- **Links:** sampled, agreeno, divdec
- **Opened:** secondary - Dorset House table of contents (dorsethouse.com/books/ercontents.html) and a course slide deck summarizing the book (slideum.com); book text not opened
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Dorset House table of contents, Marick's review and Ian Alexander's review. Dropped the '100 people cluster into a few groups' claim from a slide deck, which I could not confirm. Book text not opened.

### 24. Reward as evidence, Hadfield-Menell et al. · 2017 · ✓ confirmed

The paper treats a designed reward as an observation about what the designer wants, to be read in the context of the training environment it was designed for, and uses that to plan risk-averse behavior in new environments.

- **For the loop:** Read a score as evidence about the intended look in the cases the author imagined, and keep cautious choices for what the score never covered.
- **Links:** dirlevel, goal, specgap
- **Opened:** abstract - arXiv:1711.02827 (NeurIPS 2017)
- **Fact-check note:** Opened arXiv abstract (NeurIPS 2017). Matches.

### 25. Uncertainty keeps the switch, Hadfield-Menell et al. · 2017 · ✓ confirmed

In a game between a human and a robot, an agent that takes its reward as given has an incentive to disable the off switch unless the human is perfectly rational; an agent uncertain about the utility treats the human's actions as observations and keeps the switch.

- **For the loop:** A stop control that is also an input: a performer should read an interrupt or a rejected line as information about what you want, not as an obstacle.
- **Links:** monitor, oneq, yes
- **Opened:** abstract - arXiv:1611.08219, version 3 (2017)
- **Fact-check note:** Opened arXiv abstract (v3, June 2017). Matches.

### 26. Wrong goal out of distribution, Langosco et al. · 2022 · ✓ confirmed

In goal misgeneralization an RL agent keeps its capabilities out of distribution but pursues the wrong goal. Shah et al. add that it can happen even when the specification is correct, with examples across several kinds of deep learning systems.

- **For the loop:** A performer that passes the score on the cases shown may still be pursuing a different goal; test on a line the score never covered.
- **Links:** goal, agreeno, dirlevel
- **Opened:** abstracts - arXiv:2105.14111 (Langosco et al., ICML 2022) and arXiv:2210.01790 (Shah et al., 2022)

### 27. Penalty defaults, Ayres & Gertner · 1989 · ◐ narrower

Penalty defaults are purposefully set at what the parties would not want, to push at least one party to contract around them and reveal information. The U.C.C.'s zero-quantity default is the authors' own example: 'what we term a penalty default'.

- **For the loop:** A 'default' line could be set deliberately to something nobody wants, so a performer that keeps it has to say so.
- **Links:** statesgap, unsaid, namedef
- **Opened:** full text excerpts - Yale Law Journal 99 (1989), copy at ianayres.yale.edu and doi.org/10.2307/796722

Dropped the unverified clause that they warn against defaults that reduce useful information.

### 28. Purpose decides the rule, Fuller · 1958 · ✓ confirmed

Replying to Hart, Fuller asks whether a working World War II truck mounted on a pedestal as a war memorial falls under a 'no vehicles in the park' rule, and argues a rule cannot be applied without looking to its purpose, not just the word's core meaning.

- **For the loop:** Each fixed line should state its purpose, so a performer can see which edge cases the line was meant to cover.
- **Links:** plainlines, pscore, specgap
- **Opened:** full text excerpt - Harvard Law Review 71(4):630 (1958) (NYU copy), plus Struchiner & Hannikainen, Judgment and Decision Making 2023, and Schauer, NYU Law Review 83
- **Fact-check note:** Opened Schauer, NYU L. Rev. 83, and Struchiner & Hannikainen 2023, both quoting Fuller, 71 Harv. L. Rev. 630, 661-69. Matches.

### 29. Canons thrust and parry, Llewellyn · 1950 · ✓ confirmed

Llewellyn sets out canons of statutory construction in two columns, 'thrust but parry', saying there are two opposing canons on almost every point; to make a canon take hold, the construction must be sold by other means, such as the good sense of the situation.

- **For the loop:** A rule-style line is cheap to counter with an opposite rule; give each fixed line its reason, as the canons show a bare rule settles little.
- **Links:** plainlines, namedef, agreeno
- **Opened:** full text - Vanderbilt Law Review 3:395 (1950) (scholarship.law.vanderbilt.edu)
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the Vanderbilt Law Review full text: quote about two opposing canons and being 'sold, essentially, by means other than the use of the canon' matches.

### 30. Stock elements unprotected, scenes a faire · n.d. · ◐ narrower

Scenes a faire are elements customary in a genre and so not protectable by copyright, under the idea/expression distinction; the rationale is that letting one author monopolise them would harm later authors and the public domain.

- **For the loop:** The shared AI habits read as genre stock elements: they are expected, so nothing marks them as chosen unless the score says so.
- **Links:** unsaid, plainuni, lookholds
- **Opened:** secondary - Wikipedia 'Scenes a faire' (en.wikipedia.org); case law not opened
- **Fact-check note:** Opened a University of Michigan copyright guide and Houston Law Review article (Said 2023). Dropped the claim that specific sequences and arrangements remain protectable; not checked against case law. No date is claimed for the doctrine.

### 31. Cooperative principle, Grice · 1975 · ✓ confirmed

Grice proposes a cooperative principle with four categories of maxims (quantity, quality, relation, manner); when a speaker apparently breaks one, the hearer infers a meaning beyond what is said, called a conversational implicature.

- **For the loop:** What a score leaves unsaid is read by performers as implied; the quantity maxim says to say enough, and no more, in each score line.
- **Links:** unsaid, namedef, oneq
- **Opened:** primary excerpt - 'Logic and Conversation' (1975, sfu.ca copy) and Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 'Implicature'
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the 'Logic and Conversation' text (sfu.ca copy): cooperative principle and four categories confirmed. The flouting-to-implicature step is standard in the same paper; not re-read in full.

### 32. Prototypes and typicality, Rosch · 1975 · ◐ narrower

Rosch and Mervis found that items rated most prototypical share the most attributes with others in their category and the fewest with contrasting categories. In artificial categories, family resemblance went along with ease of learning and reaction time.

- **For the loop:** The most typical output is the cheapest to produce, which fits the one averaged taste; a performer will reach for the prototype unless the score names a different one.
- **Links:** avgtaste, flatten, signature
- **Opened:** secondary - Rosch & Mervis, 'Family Resemblances', Cognitive Psychology 7 (1975), full text; Rosch's 'Cognitive representations of semantic categories' (JEP General 104) abstract not opened
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the paper abstract and discussion. Reworded 'ratings predict performance' to 'went along with', matching the correlational result.

### 33. Seizing and freezing, Kruglanski & Webster · 1996 · ◐ narrower

The need for closure is a desire for definite knowledge, driven by an urgency tendency to reach closure quickly and a permanence tendency to maintain it as long as possible; it varies between people and situations.

- **For the loop:** A reviewer pressed for a verdict will take the first reading and hold it, so ask one question at a time and show a divergence before anyone has settled.
- **Links:** yes, oneq, divdec
- **Opened:** abstract and excerpts - Psychological Review 103(2):263-283 (PubMed 8637961; full text at kruglanskiarie.com)
- **Fact-check note:** Opened the PubMed abstract. Dropped 'seize on early information and become impervious to later data', which the abstract does not say.

## O. Medium (18)

### 34. Remediation, Bolter & Grusin · 1999 · ✓ confirmed

The authors say media are understood by how they refashion earlier media. They call this remediation and pair two logics: immediacy (the medium tries to vanish) and hypermediacy (it shows itself). They treat both as practices of specific groups and times, not universal truths.

- **For the loop:** A performer's page is a remediation of older web pages; the score should say which logic it wants (page that vanishes vs page that shows its build) so a plain-white default is a choice, not an accident.
