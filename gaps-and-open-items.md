## 7. Suggestions to be considered

### Ranked — my recommendation

1. **Give every line state an operational rule** (statesgap is still only a decision). Band: store a width and check bands together as a stack (tolerance stack-up). Open: store a trigger, the point where waiting loses an option (Poppendieck). Default: make it a penalty default that a performer must name to keep (Ayres & Gertner). Fixed: carry a purpose clause (Fuller) and a recorded way to unfix (Nomic). Rounds move open → band → fixed (set-based design).
1. **Re-aim the house-style metric:** score novelty and typicality as two numbers (Hekkert), so the plain uniform stops scoring as a win.
1. **Three cheap model-only tests, predictions first:** a base-model performer plus a Verbalized Sampling arm (separates alignment's look from the brief's); Library example early vs late vs repeated (Kulkarni); one page vs three in the swarm (Tohidi).
1. **Constraints only in pairs, dated:** every ban names its replacement (Stokes) and carries a date, because the replacement spreads (Simmel). Retire pure refusal lists (Rainer, Dogme, the plain arm).
1. **Re-cut the 60 swarm sessions as a G-study** (Cronbach): same split as m56, plus a D-study that says how many runs and judges a verdict needs. No new runs.
1. **Keep critique cross-family and blind, Braintrust-style:** same-family judges favour their own (Panickssery); one shared stronger judge can lower group quality (Kleinberg & Raghavan); critics propose, the owner decides (Catmull).
1. **Run the stranger round.** It closes q_human, q_sus, q_fix and q_flip at once and is the only test of the 25 stand-in findings.
1. **Set an attention budget per round** (q_cost): any new check from this list must replace an old one.

### Every move the round-3 reading suggests (123), by lane — candidates, not recommendations


**M. Constraint as a method**

- *Paired constraints, Stokes* (verified): Each performer's constraint should come as a pair, ban the habitual move and name its replacement, and the pair should follow from the score's goal, not be picked arbitrarily.
- *Rats and the clinamen, Oulipo* (scoped): A performer's break from a banned move should be logged as a named deviation with the compliant option it rejected, so a clinamen is a recorded choice rather than drift.
- *Less is more in art, Elster* (corrected): Mark each constraint on a score line by origin (imposed by the brief, chosen from a genre, or invented for this round), since they ask different things of a performer.
- *Constraints as freedom, Stravinsky* (verified): This is a statement from a composer, not evidence; use it as motivation for tighter score lines, and test the effect with the swatch run.
- *Obstructions as a game, von Trier & Leth* (verified): Each next constraint can be chosen after reading the diff on the previous round, as a sequence of obstructions, with a 'complete freedom' round as a deliberate control.
- *Ten rules of chastity, Dogme 95* (verified): A set of refusals gives a recognisable look (a new uniform) and was bent in practice, so list its breaks next to it, as the plain-arm result with 17 of 17 white pages suggests.
- *Cards for being stuck, Eno & Schmidt* (verified): A drawn random prompt is a cheap knob for a stuck performer, but it is a random constraint, so keep it separate from the paired constraints that come from the score.
- *Dropping a constraint, Boden* (verified): Separate rounds that explore within the current score from rounds that deliberately drop one defining line, and say which one a round was.
- *Restricted choice, Sellier & Dahl* (verified): How creative a performer feels with a wide score says nothing about its output, so judge the output blind and don't ask the performer.
- *Examples early and repeatedly, Kulkarni, Dow & Klemmer* (verified): A Library example should be shown early in the round and again after the first sketch, and the diff should count features copied from it as conformity.
- *Structured imagination, Ward* (scoped): A model asked for something new will likely carry over the defaults of its category, which fits the shared look; the ask must name which structure to break.
- *Generate, then explore, Finke, Ward & Smith* (scoped): Ask performers for rough preinventive sketches before naming the target, then interpret them against the score; the plan-first step can run in that order.
- *Keep, break, leave, Shu-ha-ri* (scoped): Treat a junior performer's first rounds as keep-the-form work and mark which score lines may be broken only after that; do not cite Rikyu as the origin.
- *Transmission by copying, Xie He* (verified): Copying a Library part is an accepted first rung, and the provenance mark should say copied; do not equate copying with the model reusing its own defaults.
- *Rasa as flavour, Natyashastra* (verified): A persona or score line can state the state to be evoked in the viewer as well as the form, which fits the Expects and Just before lines.
- *A list of refusals, Rainer* (verified): A pure list of refusals is easy to write but, like the plain arm, it can swap one uniform for another, so each refusal should name what replaces it.

**N. Tolerances, defaults and specs**

- *Bands and stack-ups, GD&T* (scoped): A 'band' line should carry its width, and the spread of several lines read together has to be checked as a stack, not line by line.
- *Sets narrowed gradually, Sobek, Ward & Liker* (verified): Letting performers return sets of variants and narrowing lines stepwise (open, then band, then fixed) matches this; the default is to commit to one point early.
- *Last responsible moment, Poppendieck* (scoped): An 'open' line needs a stated trigger, the point at which leaving it open removes an option; without one it silently becomes a 'default'.
- *Control vs noise factors, Taguchi* (scoped): Treat the brief and score as control factors and the model and run-to-run variation as noise; judge a score by whether its output holds when the performer changes.
- *Visible rules, hidden parameters, Baldwin & Clark* (scoped): Fixed lines act as visible rules for all performers; open lines are the hidden part where performers may differ without breaking the whole.
- *Hide what will change, Parnas* (verified): Mark on the score which decisions are likely to change and keep them out of what other parts depend on.
- *Ambiguity poll, Gause & Weinberg* (scoped): Count how many distinct readings independent performers give, and use the stress-each-word trick on a score line to write the questions it implies.
- *Reward as evidence, Hadfield-Menell et al.* (verified): Read a score as evidence about the intended look in the cases the author imagined, and keep cautious choices for what the score never covered.
- *Uncertainty keeps the switch, Hadfield-Menell et al.* (verified): A stop control that is also an input: a performer should read an interrupt or a rejected line as information about what you want, not as an obstacle.
- *Wrong goal out of distribution, Langosco et al.* (verified): A performer that passes the score on the cases shown may still be pursuing a different goal; test on a line the score never covered.
- *Penalty defaults, Ayres & Gertner* (scoped): A 'default' line could be set deliberately to something nobody wants, so a performer that keeps it has to say so.
- *Purpose decides the rule, Fuller* (verified): Each fixed line should state its purpose, so a performer can see which edge cases the line was meant to cover.
- *Canons thrust and parry, Llewellyn* (verified): A rule-style line is cheap to counter with an opposite rule; give each fixed line its reason, as the canons show a bare rule settles little.
- *Stock elements unprotected, scenes a faire* (scoped): The shared AI habits read as genre stock elements: they are expected, so nothing marks them as chosen unless the score says so.
- *Cooperative principle, Grice* (verified): What a score leaves unsaid is read by performers as implied; the quantity maxim says to say enough, and no more, in each score line.
- *Prototypes and typicality, Rosch* (scoped): The most typical output is the cheapest to produce, which fits the one averaged taste; a performer will reach for the prototype unless the score names a different one.
- *Seizing and freezing, Kruglanski & Webster* (scoped): A reviewer pressed for a verdict will take the first reading and hold it, so ask one question at a time and show a divergence before anyone has settled.

**O. Medium**

- *Remediation, Bolter & Grusin* (verified): A performer's page is a remediation of older web pages; the score should say which logic it wants (page that vanishes vs page that shows its build) so a plain-white default is a choice, not an accident.
- *The medium is the message, McLuhan* (verified): The medium here is the model plus the brief; sameness across pages is the model's own pattern showing through, so judge the pattern across runs, not the content of any one page.
- *Medium specificity, Greenberg* (verified): A score that names what is unique to a web page (scroll, type, link, state) gives performers a reason to use the medium, rather than the copied hero-plus-pricing shape.
- *Medium as conventions, Krauss* (scoped): The shared habits the study counted (label over headline, pricing section) behave like conventions of a medium: learned, judged against, and changeable by naming the replacement, not by hiding the support.
- *Studio as compositional tool, Eno* (verified): The performer round is additive studio work; the useful part is his remove step ('taking some of the original things off and seeing what you're left with'), which supports a subtract pass on the score.
- *Failure becomes signature, Eno* (scoped): The models' repeated tics (serif plus italic word, label above headline) may be this season's signature; ask whether each is a failure worth keeping or a default to ban.
- *Glitch as material, Menkman* (verified): Keep and show the performers' failures (console errors, broken states) as captured material, since they expose what the medium's defaults hide.
- *Kuleshov effect replications* (scoped): The context a reporter sees (the trace ending, the neighbouring page) can shift judgment of identical material, so keep the blind-and-lock step; but the size of the effect is contested, so do not quote one number.
- *Colour is relative, Albers* (verified): A judge's reading of a page depends on what sits next to it, so compare pages on identical grounds (same order-shuffled frame) before trusting a visual call.
- *Printing should be invisible, Warde* (scoped): The 'plain' arm (system fonts, white) is Warde's goblet applied to a whole page; her rule is that invisibility must serve the reader, so judge whether plain removed identity, not just defaults.
- *Playing against the apparatus, Flusser* (verified): Each model is an apparatus with a program richer than any three-performer round; the score should ask performers to play against it (ban the habitual move) and expect the round to show only part of it.
- *Variability, Manovich* (verified): Generated pages are the variability case: many versions per brief is normal, so the question is which differences are open decisions and which are the model's habit.
- *Re-recording until the room remains, Lucier* (scoped): Feeding a model's output back as the next input converges on the model's own resonances, so check chained or reused-example loops for exactly this drift.
- *Dataset as artwork, Ridler* (verified): Her own-hand dataset is a model for the material pack: real, labelled, owned inputs shape the output more than an adjective list does, and the labelling choices should be shown.
- *Language models and poetry, Parrish* (verified): Treat model output as material that a person arranges into a design; the arrangement and the stopping point are the human's, which fits the hand-and-provenance marks.
- *Voice model as instrument, Herndon* (verified): Credit and governance can be built into how material is gathered, so the provenance register should record who agreed to what at collection, not after.
- *New Alphabet, Crouwel* (scoped): Designing to the medium's real limit is a way out of defaults, but it is a statement, not a product; the plain arm's restrictions may need the same labelling.
- *Chosen limits, PICO-8* (verified): Fixed limits (a score line fixed, a band, a token budget) produce a shared look by design; a limit chosen by the author is different from a shared default, so record who chose it.

**P. Scores and performers**

- *Programmes for solutions, Gerstner* (verified): A score should state its determining elements as choices to combine, which is what the knobs on the drum machine and paired constraints already do.
- *Score and compliance, Goodman* (verified): Only the score lines that are truly checkable (present or absent on the rendered page) can be graded as compliance; the rest of a score needs a different check than yes or no.
- *Gaps the reader fills, Iser* (corrected): Where performers fill a gap differently, that gap is a place the score left open, which is why divergence can name decisions; it cannot tell a deliberate gap from an accidental one.
- *Places of indeterminacy, Ingarden* (verified): A line left open on a score is a deliberate indeterminacy, and the Sketchbook should mark it as open rather than leave it to look like an omission.
- *Negative capability, Keats* (verified): Some lines should be allowed to stay open on purpose; the loop should not force a fix on a decision the owner has not made.
- *Style as constrained choice, Meyer* (verified): A shared look is what you get when every performer inherits the same constraints and the score is silent, so the first thing to change is the constraints, not the cast.
- *Telephone Pictures, Moholy-Nagy* (verified): Authorship by specification is a recognised stance, but the Ship check should record whose hand made each part, as the map's provenance marks do.
- *RSVP Cycles, Halprin* (scoped): The loop's resources, score, judgement and performance map onto RSVP directly, so the useful borrowing is its open-to-closed gradient for score lines.
- *One percent disorder, Molnar* (scoped): A small, named dose of disorder on one score line is a cleaner knob than hoping the cast diverges on its own.
- *Same story, 99 ways, Queneau* (verified): Holding the brief fixed and varying only the manner is a ready test for how much of a look belongs to the setup.
- *Cue cards and rules, Zorn* (scoped): The prompter role fits the STATE.md channel: players raise cues, one agent accepts or declines, and nobody gets to ask twice at once.
- *Sign vocabularies, Morris and Thompson* (scoped): A small fixed set of signs, each with a known meaning, is the model for line states: the steering stays live without free text.
- *Scenario and lazzi, Commedia* (corrected): A Library of tested pieces for the performers to draw on is old practice, with the old risk that everyone reaches for the same joke.
- *Stock schemata, Gjerdingen* (verified): A shared stock of patterns gives a recognisable period style without any one source copied, so a house look can be measured by which schemata recur.
- *Rules as constraints, Conditional Design* (verified): A difference between performers only counts as a decision if there is a reason for it on the score.
- *Instructions realised by others, do it* (verified): A set of short instructions realised at several venues is a standing example of what the same score gives across performers.
- *Event scores, Brecht and Ono* (verified): Plain short lines can carry a whole piece, which agrees with the finding that plain statements move choices as much as quotes.
- *Offers and blocking, Johnstone* (verified): An agent trained to accept every offer is the 'optimises for yes' risk in the map, so the channel needs a way to refuse that is not blocking.
- *Linked verse and the folded page, Renga* (scoped): Hiding what others did is a deliberate rule, and it fits blind-first judgment; renga is the opposite case, a chain where each link is seen.

**Q. Sketches, alternatives and measures**

- *Loose sketches widen the search, Goel* (scoped): A performer asked for a rough, unresolved first pass may explore more than one asked for a finished page; the plan-first step should stay sketch-grade.
- *What a sketch is, Buxton* (verified): Plan-first output and Sketchbook lines should read as proposals with deliberate gaps, not as finished pages that confirm a choice.
- *One design flatters, three invite criticism, Tohidi et al.* (verified): Show every reviewer or swarm persona three performer outputs side by side, not one; a lone page will be scored too kindly.
- *Parallel beats serial, Dow et al.* (scoped): Run performers in parallel on the same score and give feedback on all of them at once, not one after another.
- *Seeing anew as computation, Stiny* (verified): A performer's unexpected reading of a score line may be a resource to name, not an error; the Sketchbook should let a surprise be kept as a decision.
- *A grammar of the prairie house, Koning & Eizenberg* (verified): A style can be written as rules, so a Library entry could carry a few generative rules, not only an example, and rules are what a ban-and-replace knob needs.
- *Four makers of constraints, Lawson* (scoped): Each score line could say who set it (you, the client brief, a rule) so that fixed, band and open mean something about who may change it.
- *Problem and solution grow together, Dorst & Cross* (verified): A score that never changes after a first build cannot show the problem being reframed; expect performers to expose missing lines and allow the score to be amended between rounds.
- *Split the score variance, Cronbach et al.* (verified): Score a fixed set of pages with several judges and occasions, split the variance, then project how many judges or runs a verdict needs, instead of trusting one pass.
- *A map of the best per cell, Mouret & Clune* (verified): A Library or board could hold the best page per cell of a few chosen traits, such as layout type and colour band, instead of one winner.
- *Diversity as effective count, Friedman & Dieng* (verified): Score a round of performer pages with a similarity function you choose, to get a single diversity count that can be compared with the swatch spread.
- *Ask for the distribution, Zhang et al.* (verified): A performer could be asked for five readings of a score with stated odds, as a cheaper way to expose its habit than five full builds.
- *Entropy over meanings, Farquhar et al.* (verified): Cluster performer outputs by what decision they make, not by wording; a split across clusters marks an open line, and agreement does not prove it is settled.
- *Do models see two readings, Min et al. and Liu et al.* (verified): Do not trust a model to say a brief is ambiguous; use performer divergence to find open lines and let a person write the one question.
- *Novel but typical, Hekkert et al.* (verified): Score a page for how typical and how novel it is as two separate numbers, since a single liking score will hide that a safe look and a fresh look pull against each other.
- *Aligned models get predictable, West & Potts* (verified): A base-model performer may be worth trying as a diverging arm; judge whether its pages are original or just broken before counting it as variety.

**R. Taste, monoculture and organisations**

- *Algorithmic monoculture, Kleinberg & Raghavan* (verified): Three performers sharing one model or one score can look more reliable while the round as a whole loses range, so a shared stronger performer is not a free gain.
- *Outcome homogenization, Bommasani et al.* (verified): If two performers share a base model but differ in setup, the same pages may still fail the same way, so check how each was adapted before counting them as independent.
- *Self-recognition and self-preference, Panickssery, Bowman & Feng* (scoped): A critic from the same family as a performer may favour its work, so hide authorship and prefer a critic from another family when scores are locked.
- *Imitation and distinction, Simmel* (verified): The season's default look is a mark of what has become common; a line that bans it will need renewing as the replacement itself spreads, so a banned list should be dated.
- *MusicLab, Salganik, Dodds & Watts* (verified): Showing a performer or critic earlier results before it judges can lock in an early winner, which is why scores are given blind and in shuffled order.
- *Mere exposure, Zajonc* (scoped): Seeing the same house style on every board may raise its appeal to the judge, so a reviewer who has looked at many pages needs an unfamiliar reference to calibrate against.
- *Aesthetic concepts, Sibley* (verified): A checklist of countable items cannot certify that a page is balanced or calm, so a person still has to look at the rendered page, with the count shown beside it.
- *Blind variation and selection, Campbell and Simonton* (verified): More performers per score means more variants to select from, but selection is the part that needs a judge; do not read output volume as a quality guarantee.
- *Exploration and exploitation, March* (scoped): A library that every performer learns from quickly will converge; keep some performers on a fresh or unlearned start so the shared code keeps something to learn from.
- *Network structure of exploration, Lazer & Friedman* (verified): Letting performers see each other's work early speeds agreement and costs range; show the diff after each has produced a first pass, not before.
- *Uncertainty and equivocality, Daft & Lengel* (verified): When performers diverge it may be an ambiguous brief, not missing facts, so the fix is a question to the operator, not more material.
- *Boundary objects, Star & Griesemer* (verified): STATE.md and the score can serve as a boundary object between operator, performers and critics: one shared record each reads differently, with the same identity.
- *Garbage can model, Cohen, March & Olsen* (verified): A loop with loose preferences and changing participants will attach solutions to whichever question is open, so each open decision needs a named question before a performer is pointed at it.
- *Loose coupling, Weick* (scoped): Keep performers, critics and the operator loosely coupled through a few fixed artifacts, so one can change without forcing the others to.
- *Mangle of practice, Pickering* (scoped): A rendered page that fails to do what the score asked is the material pushing back; the performer's change in response is the useful record, so log both.
- *Material engagement theory, Malafouris* (verified): Working on real material, such as rendered pages and a placeholder register, shapes the maker's thinking, so juniors and agents who never touch the material may not build the same judgement.

**S. Buildings, play and teams**

- *Both-and, Venturi* (scoped): Two performers differing on a line can be kept as both-and on the score, but only where Venturi's condition holds: the whole must still read as one truth, so a kept difference needs a stated reason.
- *Half a house, Aravena / Elemental* (verified): A 'fixed' line is the built half and an 'open' line the half left for the owner; the loop should say which half each part is, and size the open half so it can be filled without breaking the fixed one.
- *Layers change at different rates, Brand* (scoped): Library, board, score and STATE.md move at different speeds; keep fast things (performer output) from rewriting slow ones (fixed lines), and give each layer its own review rate.
- *Super Normal, Fukasawa & Morrison* (verified): A plain, unnoticed result is not automatically the default look; judge pages by long-use performance on the score, and treat a deliberate quiet as a decision rather than an absence.
- *Grid as ordering system, Müller-Brockmann* (scoped): A grid is a constraint the designer chooses, so shared grid tokens make pages look alike on purpose; if the loop fixes a grid, mark it fixed on the score line so sameness there is a decision.
- *A few typefaces, Vignelli* (scoped): A small, declared typeface set is a legitimate fixed line, not a flaw: the sameness to worry about is the unchosen default, so require the score to say which type is fixed and why.
- *Identity as a generator, MIT Media Lab & Casa da Música* (scoped): Variation is cheap to generate and only meaningful when a rule limits it; give performers a seeded variant space and read the spread as a map of which decisions the score left open.
- *Voluntary obstacles, Suits* (verified): A constraint on a performer is only play if accepted for the activity; state each fixed line as a rule that serves the work, and keep it removable if nobody can say what it makes possible.
- *Paidia to ludus, Caillois* (verified): A score line can sit between loose and rule-bound; open lines are paidia and fixed lines ludus, and rounds could go from the first to the second as decisions settle.
- *Eleven uncertainties, Costikyan* (scoped): Divergence among performers could be sorted by kind of uncertainty (taste, solving, hidden brief), because only some kinds are open decisions for the operator.
- *Rules as a move, Suber* (verified): Fixed lines should be amendable by an explicit step, like transmutation, never silently; add a move on the score to unfix a line, recorded in STATE.md.
- *De-scription, Akrich* (verified): Each score carries a hidden script about who uses the page; test it against real users, since stand-in rounds only show the script back.
- *Bricoleur vs engineer, Lévi-Strauss* (scoped): A Library is a bricoleur's stock: pieces valued for possible use, not made for one brief; keep them half-specialized and tag each part with where it came from.
- *Training for uncertainty, Fox* (verified): Debriefs and score lines should say whether a gap is the performer's limit or the brief's; where it can't be told, mark the line open rather than blaming either.
- *Mixed-initiative principles, Horvitz* (scoped): Decide whether an agent acts, asks or does nothing by the cost of a wrong guess; ask one question only where an error costs more than interrupting.
- *Human-AI guidelines, Amershi et al.* (verified): Use the guidelines as a checklist on the STATE.md channel and Sketchbook, e.g. scoping services when in doubt, but remember 'validated' means practitioners judged relevance.
- *Automation as team player, Klein et al.* (verified): The loop needs a visible compact: who does what, what each agent will do next, how the operator redirects, and a signal when a performer cannot finish.
- *Traces in a shared medium, Grassé & Heylighen* (verified): STATE.md, the score and the page itself can be the medium: each agent leaves a trace the next reads, with line states as the visible marks.
- *Blackboard, Erman et al. (Hearsay-II)* (verified): A shared board with levels and a credibility mark per entry is a close model for line states, with a scheduler (the operator) deciding which entry to work next.
- *Critical Response Process, Lerman* (verified): Critique rounds can hold the performer as questioner and ask permission before opinions; neutral questions about a page come before any verdict.
- *Braintrust, Catmull* (verified): A critic that proposes but cannot mandate keeps the performer or operator as owner; mark critique output as notes, never as changes landed.
