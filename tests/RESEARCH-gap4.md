# Gap 4: instruments for visual similarity, typicality and novelty of rendered pages

Read depth key: FULL = substantial body text opened via search/fetch excerpts; ABS = abstract only; SEC = secondary page. "Excerpt" means I saw long passages but not the whole paper. Nothing below is from memory unless marked NOT READ.

## 1. Homogenization of web design
**Goree, Doosti, Crandall, Su (2021), CHI '21, "Investigating the Homogenization of Web Design".** Read: abstract plus ACM full-HTML excerpts (methods, section 4.1). Measured: pairwise visual distance between website screenshots 2003-2019 (Internet Archive snapshots; "Dense Russell" subset of 100 sites, every 15 days). Method: three distances: CNN features (a net trained to classify which of 100 companies a snapshot belongs to), colour (CIEL*c*h* histograms + Earth Mover's Distance), layout (XY-tree + tree edit distance). Numbers read: color distance down 32% 2008-2019; layout down 44% 2010-2019 (the contributions list says 43%; the abstract says "over 30%"); CNN down 30% 2007-2019; 11 designer interviews. Authors state they "cannot claim our measures of similarity are definitive"; I saw no human-judgment validation of the distances. Means for us: a three-channel design (learned + colour + layout) is the precedent; layout distance moved most, so measure layout separately from colour.

## 2. Computational aesthetics validated against people
- **Reinecke et al. (2013), CHI, "Predicting users' first impressions of website aesthetics with a quantification of perceived visual complexity and colorfulness".** Read: abstract + PDF excerpt. 450 sites, 548 volunteers for ratings. Computational model vs mean perceived complexity r = .80 (R2 = .65). Appeal study: 242 volunteers, complexity + colorfulness + demographics explain adj. R2 = .48. Colorfulness only minor for appeal; the two models correlate weakly. Means: screenshot-only complexity/colour features are cheap, human-validated covariates, but they predict appeal, not "house look".
- **Miniukovich & De Angeli (2015), CHI, "Computation of Interface Aesthetics".** Read: abstract. Eight automatic metrics; webpages N=62, iPhone apps N=53; 150 ms and 4 s exposures; regression explains up to 49% (web) and 32% (apps, with genre) of aesthetics variance. Predecessor, AVI 2014 "Quantification of interface visual complexity": up to 51% of aesthetics, 50% of complexity; list of determinants includes "prototypicality" (read in abstract only, no number).
- **Lindgaard et al. (2006), Behaviour & Information Technology 25(2), "50 milliseconds".** Read: abstract + results excerpt. Appeal ratings at 50 ms vs 500 ms correlate r = .947 (mean-based). Inter-rater average correlation was only .337/.403 at 50 ms vs .557/.599 at 500 ms: the group mean is stable, individual raters are not. Means: use several raters and aggregate; a single rater at short exposure is noisy.
- Successors beyond these: NOT READ.

## 3. Perceptual image similarity vs humans
- **Zhang et al. (2018), CVPR, LPIPS / BAPPS.** Read: PDF excerpts. 2AFC + JND judgments, 5 judgments per triplet in validation sets; humans score p^2+(1-p)^2 in expectation. Patch-level distortions, not layouts.
- **Fu et al. (2023), NeurIPS, DreamSim / NIGHTS.** Read: arXiv full-HTML excerpt incl. Table 5. 20k synthetic triplets with unanimous human votes. 2AFC alignment: PSNR 57.1, SSIM 57.3, LPIPS 70.7, DISTS 86.0, CLIP B/32 83.1, OpenCLIP B/32 87.6, DINO B/16 90.1, ensemble 90.8; tuned DreamSim 96.2 (96.16 in text). Authors say existing metrics miss "mid-level" layout/semantic similarity, and DreamSim "is sensitive to color and layout". Caveat: triplets were filtered to be unanimous, natural/synthetic images, not web pages; ranking may differ on screenshots. Means: for off-the-shelf similarity pick DINO or OpenCLIP over LPIPS; DreamSim is the strongest human-aligned option but unvalidated on UI.

## 4. UI representations and diversity of generated UI/images
- **Design2Code (Si et al., NAACL 2025).** Read: arXiv HTML excerpt. Metrics: CLIP-B/32 similarity of screenshots (text inpainted), block-match, text, position, CIEDE2000 colour. Logistic regression on metric differences predicted human pairwise preference at 79.9% (435 pairs); text similarity had a negative, least-significant coefficient. Means: humans react to layout and colour, not text, so mask text before embedding.
- **UIClip (Wu et al., 2024), UIST.** Read: abstract + method excerpt. CLIP B/32 fine-tuned on UIs; 12 designers, ~1.2K ratings, 892 usable pairs; Krippendorff alpha = 0.37 on 10 shared pairs. Means: a quality scorer, not a similarity measure, but its embedding is UI-specific; designer agreement on quality is low.
- **UICrit (Duan et al., UIST 2024).** Read: excerpts. 983 UIs, 3,059 critiques, 7 designers; Fleiss kappa 0.30 and 0.29 ("fair") on critique-quality ratings.
- **Screen2Vec (Li et al., CHI 2021).** ABS only. Embeds text, layout and app context from interaction traces; no numbers read. It targets function, not look.
- **Design Creativity Bench (Bhole et al., arXiv 2610.11539, Oct 2026; preprint).** Read: abstract, method and appendix B5 excerpts. LLM-generated UI vs human designs: same-prompt different-model originality 0.592 vs human-model 0.764; creative range 0.581 vs human 0.902. Closest published match to our problem. Human validation of the similarity score: 100 cases, 3 reviewers, pick which of two pairs is more similar; reviewer-pair agreement 82.1/79.4/93.4% (mean 85.0%). Agreement of score with pooled label (80 non-tied cases): raw UIClip whole-page .738 (AUC .765), Gemini embedding whole-page .613, UIClip + fitted projection .787, hybrid segment-wise + projection .825 (AUC .869), best LLM judge .885. Tiny n (80); no CIs given in what I read. Means: raw embeddings of whole pages are weak (.61-.74); segmenting the page and learning a projection helps; relative "which pair is more similar" labels are the workable human protocol.
- **"Machine-made websites converge on each other" (ai-design-convergence.vercel.app).** Read: full page. Blog, not peer reviewed. 945 award-winning sites measured in Chromium at 1440x900 after `document.fonts.ready`, 14 DOM/style features. Median human-human distance 4.45; between AI outputs 1.91-3.53 (3 pairwise distances only). Typicality as z-distance from the human centroid: two AI outputs 2.16 and 2.34 vs human median 3.01. Four of five tools produced warm ground + serif display + brass + uppercase letterspaced labels. Cluster purity vs source gallery 0.29-0.35 used as a control. Means: the exact rendered-DOM approach, with a built-in null (gallery purity); but one brief, n=3.
- **Vendi Score (Friedman & Dieng; arXiv 2210.02410).** ABS. exp(entropy of similarity-matrix eigenvalues); takes any similarity function. **Okamoto et al. (2024, J. Japanese Soc. AI)**: Vendi had the highest correlation with human diversity judgments among metrics tried (ABS only; no number read). **Graham et al. 2026 (Zenodo)** (ABS): CLIP and DINOv2 effective dimension; two commercial T2I models converge at cosine 0.86 on identical prompts vs 0.64 mismatched-prompt baseline. Means: report diversity as an effective number, and always include a mismatched baseline.

## 5. Typicality vs novelty
- **Hekkert, Snelders, van Wieringen (2003), Brit. J. Psychology 94, "Most advanced, yet acceptable".** Read: abstract + a secondary summary (jimdavies.org). Typicality ("goodness of example") and novelty are strongly negatively correlated as rated, and suppress each other's link to preference; effects appear only when each is partialled out. Study 3: objective typicality = an item's mean similarity to all others in the category ("central tendency"), validated against human ratings; summary says it correlated "very strongly" with rated typicality (no number read).
- **Mayer & Landwehr (2017), Design Studies, "Objective measures of design typicality".** Read: abstract-level excerpt. 77 car models: objective vs rated typicality r = .501 (manual feature points), .321/.312 (image correlation), .307 (algorithmic feature points). Even the best objective measure explains ~25% of rated typicality.
- **Keeler et al. (ASME 2025) and thesis.** Excerpts only: novelty as distance from the centroid of a human-triplet embedding; they test whether language-model embeddings can substitute. No validated recipe for splitting the two found.
- Conclusion: I found no source that validates a method for keeping typicality and novelty apart. The literature says they are negatively correlated in human ratings too (Hekkert), so r = -0.87 in TF-IDF is partly construct, not just artefact. Separable operationalisations exist: typicality = mean similarity to the whole category (centroid or mean pairwise); novelty = distance to nearest neighbour (or to a reference corpus the generator never saw). These differ in dense vs sparse regions but I found no human-validated test of the pair on web pages.

## 6. Inter-rater methods for visual attributes
- Visual-quality tasks reach only "fair" agreement: UICrit Fleiss 0.29-0.30; UIClip alpha 0.37. Relative forced-choice similarity does better: DesignCreativityBench 85% raw agreement (not chance-corrected, no kappa reported); DreamSim only kept triplets with unanimous votes and dropped ~20% of workers failing sentinels.
- Practices seen: rubric with defined factors (UICrit), sentinel items (DreamSim, LPIPS), aggregating multiple raters (Lindgaard, Design2Code 5 raters majority vote), reporting pairwise range not only mean (measuretheweb.org advice: Fleiss/Krippendorff for >2 raters; mean pairwise kappa hides range). I found no source giving kappa for named style attributes such as "serif display with italic word"; that is a gap.

## Recommendation
Stack: (1) Rendered-DOM rules (computed fonts, backgrounds, bbox layout, after fonts ready, fixed viewport) for countable habits, the Chromium approach in the convergence blog and Goree's hand-engineered colour/layout channels. (2) For similarity, embed a text-masked viewport screenshot plus per-section crops with DINO or OpenCLIP (best off-the-shelf in DreamSim), optionally DreamSim; add a UIClip embedding as a UI-specific alternative. (3) Typicality = mean similarity to a held-out human-designed reference corpus (Hekkert central tendency); novelty = nearest-neighbour distance to that corpus plus to sibling generations; report them as two numbers and show their correlation rather than combining. (4) Diversity across a batch as Vendi score with a mismatched-prompt baseline. (5) Validate against blind human labels: forced-choice "which pair is more similar" (about 100 cases, 3+ raters, sentinels), plus a small yes/no panel for each house-look habit, reporting Krippendorff alpha or Fleiss with intervals and per-pair range.

Failure modes: embeddings tuned on natural images are unvalidated on pages, whole-page embeddings scored .61-.74 against humans in the one UI test I read; content/topic leaks into similarity (control for the brief); very tall pages get squashed to 224px; typicality depends on which reference corpus you choose; typicality and novelty are genuinely anti-correlated so a high r is not proof of a bug; tiny human validation sets (n=80) give wide intervals; visual-attribute kappa will likely stay low unless attributes are binary, anchored with examples and rated on cropped regions.
